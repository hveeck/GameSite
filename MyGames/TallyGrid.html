Rules & Scoring Guide

Objective & Playing
Play on a 5×5 grid with 1–4 players. The game uses one shuffled 54-card deck: 52 standard cards and 2 Jokers, with no replacement. Take turns placing one card from your hand in an empty space touching a card already on the board by an edge (up, down, left, or right), then draw a replacement card when available. Diagonal-only contact does not count. The initial board varies with your chosen layout. In multiplayer, the highest total score when the board fills wins; in solo, maximize your final total.
Card Values — Both Scoring Modes
Ace = 1. Number cards 2–10 are worth their printed number. Jack, Queen, and King = 10 points each when adding card values for 15 and 31. For runs, ranks are A, 2, 3, …, 10, J, Q, K in that order (Ace is low). For pairs, cards must match rank, not just value: J and K are both worth 10 in sums but do not form a pair. Suits are ♠, ♥, ♦, ♣.
General Scoring
You score combinations created or extended by the card you just placed; a combination must include that new card. You may earn several awards on a single move (such as a 15 and a pair). Scores are awarded immediately; combinations already sitting on the board are not scored again unless they form a new qualifying combination involving your played card. The same card can take part in multiple distinct combinations.
Classic Scoring — Straight Rows or Columns
Every scoring combination must occupy consecutive, uninterrupted cells in the same single row or column, including your new card. No gaps, bends, branching, or diagonal connections. Flushes never score in Classic.
  • 15 = 3 points: Each distinct consecutive segment of at least two cards totaling exactly 15. Example: 7 + 8 = 15; J + 5 = 15 because J is worth 10.
  • Pairs and sets: Consecutive identical ranks in the same row/column earn 2 points for 2, 6 for 3, 12 for 4, or 15 for 5. Different suits can pair. Example: 9♠ next to 9♥ makes a pair; J and Q do not.
  • Runs: 3 consecutive cells showing consecutive ranks in ascending or descending spatial order earn 4 points; 4 cards earn 8; 5 earn 12. Example: 4–5–6 scores; 5–4–6 does not. A smaller run fully contained within a longer scored run is not counted again.
  • 31 = 6 points: All currently occupied cards in the entire row or column must total exactly 31 (even if one or more spaces are still empty). You cannot skip an occupied card to make 31. Filling an empty space later does not undo previously earned points. Each row and each column can award a 31 bonus only once during the entire game, regardless of which player earned it. A new 31 on an already-claimed row/column earns no second bonus.
Expanded Scoring — Connected Shapes
For 15s, runs, and flushes, cards may form any orthogonally connected group that includes the newly placed card: a chain, bend, T, or branch all qualify. Every card in the group must be reachable from another group card through edge-to-edge touches. Diagonals alone do not connect. These connected groups contain 2–5 cards for 15s and 3–5 for runs/flushes. Cards need not appear in rank order on the grid for an Expanded run.
  • 15 = 3 points: Every distinct connected group of 2–5 cards totaling exactly 15. Example: a 7 and 8 touching on a corner-shaped chain still score if the cards of the chosen group connect edge-to-edge.
  • Runs: A connected group containing 3, 4, or 5 distinct consecutive ranks in any arrangement earns 4, 8, or 12 points. Example: 5–4–6 can score in Expanded as long as the three cards form a connected group. A contained shorter run is not awarded again when its longer run scores.
  • Flushes: 3, 4, or 5 connected cards all of the same suit earn 2, 4, or 8 points. Contained smaller flushes are not also awarded when a larger flush scores. A Queen uses its printed suit, not a wildcard suit.
  • Pairs and sets: Unlike the three combinations above, pairs remain straight and consecutive in a single row or column. Exact matching rank awards 2/6/12/15 points for 2/3/4/5 cards.
  • 31 = 6 points: The values of every currently occupied card in the entire row or column must total exactly 31. Empty spaces are allowed, but you cannot omit occupied cards or score a subsection. The bonus may be collected only once per row and once per column across the whole game.
J/Q/K Court Powers — Optional
The J/Q/K Court Powers setup switch starts Off. With powers Off, J/Q/K are regular cards (value 10 for sums; J, Q, K as their respective ranks in runs and sets). When powers are On:
  • Jack — Suit Strike: In addition to regular scoring, gain +3 points per card directly adjacent above/below/left/right that shares the Jack’s printed suit. Diagonals do not count.
  • Queen — Wild Run: A Queen may stand for a missing rank in a run only. In Classic its position must fit the straight ascending/descending run; in Expanded it can stand for a needed rank anywhere in the connected group. It is still worth 10 for 15/31, counts as rank Q for pairs, and uses its actual printed suit for Expanded flushes.
  • King — The Usurper: Play normally into an empty valid cell, or once per player per game use a King to overwrite an existing A–10 card on the board, removing that old card. The King keeps its printed rank, suit, and sum value of 10; it is not a wildcard. A King cannot overwrite J, Q, K, or Joker cards.
Jokers & Hand Visibility
Joker — Chameleon: When you play either Joker, choose any A–10 rank and one of the four suits. It then scores using those chosen properties (the chosen rank also determines its value for 15 and 31). Jokers are present regardless of whether court powers are enabled. Open Hands shows the bots’ cards; Closed Hands hides them. Neither changes how points are calculated.
