A BEGINNER'S FIELD GUIDE

How to Play

All three games are turn-based. Select your bot level, choose your side, and use the highlighted squares to make a legal move. These rules are enough to get started; each game also has a detailed Rules & History button.

01 · MESOPOTAMIA

The Royal Game of Ur

Goal: Be first to move all seven of your pieces from the start, along the road, and off the board.

  1. Roll the four dice. Each die shows either a marked or unmarked face. Count the marked faces: that's how many spaces you move (0–4).
  2. Choose one piece. Click a piece already on the road, or click your reserve to bring a new piece in. Then click its highlighted destination.
  3. Capture by landing. On the shared middle road, landing on an enemy sends that piece back to its starting reserve. You can never land on your own piece.
  4. Use the flowers ✹. Rosette squares are safe from capture. Landing on one gives you another throw immediately.
  5. Finish exactly. To take a piece off the board, your dice must roll the exact number of remaining steps. The first player to take off all seven wins.

Uses the widely played Irving Finkel reconstruction. If you roll zero, or cannot make any legal move, your turn ends.

02 · VIKING AGE

Hnefatafl

Goal: The defenders help their king escape to a corner. The attackers try to capture him before he gets there.

  1. Know your army. The attackers have 24 dark warriors around the edges. The defenders have 12 light warriors and one king in the center. The attackers move first.
  2. Move in straight lines. Every piece, including the king, moves any number of empty squares horizontally or vertically — like a rook in chess. Pieces cannot jump.
  3. Capture by sandwiching. Move one of your warriors so an enemy warrior is caught immediately between two of yours, or between your warrior and a hostile corner or throne. The trapped piece is removed.
  4. Special squares matter. Only the king may stop on the center throne or the four corners. Other pieces may cross the empty throne.
  5. Win differently. Defenders win the instant the king reaches a corner. Attackers win by surrounding the king on four sides (three if he is next to the throne). The king cannot be captured on the outer edge.

Based on Copenhagen rules, including shieldwall captures along an edge. Two unusual referee decisions (exit forts and complete-ring encirclement) are not automatically adjudicated; the in-game guide explains them.

03 · MEDIEVAL EUROPE

Rithmomachia

Goal: Form a mathematical pattern of three pieces in the opponent's half of the board. In Classic mode, you can also win by capturing eight enemy pieces.

  1. Move by shape. Circles move one square up, down, left, or right. Triangles move exactly two squares diagonally. Squares move exactly three squares in any straight direction. No jumping.
  2. The pyramid is special. It is made of numbered layers. It can move like whichever shapes remain in its stack.
  3. Capture with numbers. You do not move onto enemy pieces. Instead, after moving, your piece can remove enemies through matching values, sums or differences with another friendly piece, distance multiplication, or a complete surrounding siege.
  4. Make a harmony. Place three equally spaced friendly pieces in a straight line entirely inside the enemy's half, with their numbers forming an arithmetic sequence (4, 6, 8), geometric sequence (4, 8, 16), or harmonic sequence (6, 8, 12).
  5. Choose your victory rule. Classic: harmony or eight captures. Pure Harmony: only the pattern wins. Capture Contest: first to capture 12 enemies.

Rithmomachia had many historical rule variants. This version uses the Renaissance-style movement and a streamlined, consistently enforced capture system. The in-game guide details each capture method.
